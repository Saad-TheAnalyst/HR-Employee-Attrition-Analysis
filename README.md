# HR Employee Attrition Analysis Dashboard

## Overview
A comprehensive analysis of IBM HR Employee Attrition 
data using Python, Pandas, Matplotlib and Power BI 
to uncover why employees leave and identify key 
attrition patterns across departments, job roles, 
age groups and salary levels.

## Tools Used
- Python
- Pandas
- Matplotlib
- Microsoft Excel
- Power BI
- JupyterLab

## Questions Answered
- How many employees left vs stayed?
- Which department has the highest attrition?
- Which job role has the highest attrition?
- Which age group leaves the most?
- How does salary affect attrition?
- Does overtime affect attrition?
- Does work life balance affect attrition?
- Does job satisfaction affect attrition?
- How does gender affect attrition by department?

## Key Findings

### Overall Attrition
- Overall attrition rate was 16.1% with 237 out 
  of 1,470 employees leaving the company.
- Average monthly income was $6,502.93.
- Average years at company was 7 years.

### Age Group Analysis
- Employees aged 18-25 had the highest attrition 
  rate at 34.78% — youngest employees leave the most.
- Employees aged 36-45 had the lowest attrition 
  rate at just 9.19%.
- Attrition decreases significantly after age 25 
  showing younger employees are harder to retain.

### Salary Analysis
- Employees who left earned an average of $4,787 
  per month compared to $6,832 for those who stayed.
- This $2,045 income gap strongly suggests salary 
  is a key driver of attrition.
- Managers earn the most at $17,181 per month 
  while Sales Representatives earn the least at $2,626.

### Department Analysis
- Sales department had the highest attrition rate 
  at 20.6% followed by Human Resources at 19%.
- Research and Development had the lowest attrition 
  rate at 13.8%.

### Job Role Analysis
- Sales Representatives had the highest attrition 
  rate at 39.8% — almost 4 in 10 leave.
- Research Directors had the lowest attrition 
  rate at just 2.5%.
- Laboratory Technicians had a 23.9% attrition 
  rate making them the second most at risk role.

### Overtime Impact
- Employees who work overtime have a 30.53% 
  attrition rate compared to just 10.44% for 
  those who do not.
- This means overtime employees are 3x more 
  likely to leave the company.

### Work Life Balance
- Employees with poor work life balance had the 
  highest attrition rate at 31.25%.
- Employees with better work life balance had 
  significantly lower attrition rates.

### Job Satisfaction
- Employees with low job satisfaction had a 
  22.84% attrition rate.
- Employees with very high job satisfaction had 
  only 11.33% attrition rate — half as much.

### Gender Analysis
- Female HR employees had the highest attrition 
  rate at 30% compared to 13.95% for males.
- Sales attrition was almost equal between 
  genders at 20.11% female and 21.01% male.

## Key Recommendations
- Increase salaries for Sales Representatives 
  who earn the least but leave the most.
- Reduce overtime requirements especially in 
  Sales and HR departments.
- Improve work life balance policies to reduce 
  the 31.25% attrition rate among poor balance employees.
- Focus retention efforts on employees in their 
  first 2 years when attrition is highest at 34-36%.
